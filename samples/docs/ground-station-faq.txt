Ground Station Operations FAQ

What is a pass?
A pass is the window during which a satellite is above the horizon of a ground station and
can exchange data with it. Low-earth-orbit passes usually last between 6 and 12 minutes.
Operators schedule passes a week ahead in the operations calendar.

How much data can we download in one pass?
The standard S-band link supports 2 megabits per second downlink, so a 10-minute pass moves
about 150 megabytes. The X-band upgrade available at the Svalbard and Punta Arenas stations
raises this to 150 megabits per second.

What happens if a pass fails?
The pass is marked as failed in the calendar and the scheduler automatically books the next
available pass for the same satellite, usually within 90 minutes. Customers are notified by
email if two consecutive passes fail. Two consecutive failed passes also open a SEV2 incident.

Which stations does Acme Orbital operate?
Acme Orbital operates seven ground stations: Svalbard, Punta Arenas, Hartebeesthoek, Perth,
Fairbanks, Awarua and Inuvik. Svalbard sees every polar-orbiting satellite on almost every
orbit and is therefore the busiest station.

How are commands authorised?
Every command sent to a satellite is signed with the customer's command key and verified by
the ground station before uplink. Commands that change orbit or power configuration require a
second operator to countersign within five minutes, otherwise the command expires.

Can customers get raw telemetry?
Yes. Raw frames are published to the telemetry.raw topic and exported to the customer's
storage bucket within 15 minutes of the end of the pass. Decoded telemetry is available in
the customer portal after processing, typically within 30 minutes.

Who do I contact about a missed pass?
Contact the Mission Operations desk, which is staffed around the clock. For anything affecting
command uplink, page the on-call engineer directly instead of waiting for the desk.
